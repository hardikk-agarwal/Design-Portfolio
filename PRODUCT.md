# Hardik Agarwal: Full Circle

## Register

brand

## Users

Design leaders and interviewers hiring for senior product and AI design roles. Visitors should meet Hardik as a person, understand his Microsoft role and hands-on practice, and know how to reach his work. A quick review should reveal the problem, ownership, and result without requiring visitors to learn the room interactions.

## Product Purpose

A personal, scroll-led portfolio rooted in Hardik's own story: growing up exploring and customizing Windows, then contributing to it as a designer at Microsoft. His photographs, point of view on communication, selected work, and interests form one sequence. The person comes first, with direct access to work and resume throughout.

## Brand Personality

Curious, responsible, and collaborative. Hardik wants to be remembered for passion, punctuality, ownership, and leadership. The narrative expresses these through his full-circle story and emphasis on shared understanding rather than unsupported superlatives.

## Voice

- First person everywhere except the cover notes, which read as third-person redlines on a design comp ("Thinks in systems").
- Meaning over inventory: say what an interest or tool does for the work. Never list gear, brands, or tools as personality; tools belong in toolkit lists.
- Lead with what Hardik owned and what changed, one proof point per claim, without repeating the same figure in every section. Keep the resume's attribution ("contributed to", "helped") and approximate figures.
- Specific and plain: no em dashes, buzzwords, restated headings, or intros that describe the layout. Sentence case for roles and labels.

## Visual Direction

Current direction (1 October 2026, fresh React rebuild): a cinematic, editorial type-and-photo portfolio. The landing is an annotated magazine cover. Hardik chose the cover over cinemagraph and Windows lock-screen previews, then felt the full-bleed bench photo read like a dating profile; from poster, manifesto, and cutout previews he chose design-comp annotations, and asked for a calmer background while keeping the bench so he is not floating. The bench portrait now fills the screen against a plain painted wall, the name runs behind his head through a registered cutout, and rose notes pin his skills, research, AI prototyping tools, interests, and a Windows Hello result to parts of the photo. On large screens the photograph then contracts into a single window while the Windows full-circle statement arrives on either side. The period in "Selected work." opens like a camera aperture onto the first project, and the three case studies stack as full-screen colour sheets with real product screens. A ThreeUI Typography Vortex closes the page. Mona Sans (variable width and weight) is the only family. Off-black and cool off-white themes share one rose accent; project colours stay inside their sheets. Design variance 8, motion intensity 8, visual density 3. Lenis smooths wheel scrolling for fine pointers only; keyboard, touch, and reduced motion keep native scrolling. The earlier windows-and-panes story below is preserved at story.html.

Previous direction: a personal type-and-photo story built around windows and shared perspective. The desktop opening composes a foreground portrait cutout, two photographic windows, and separated name typography across one unframed canvas. Scrolling moves the type and secondary windows away as the intact portrait expands. Four photographic panes then assemble the same image through the Windows origin story. A lime shared-perspective scene pairs expressive display type with the camera portrait. Project transitions expand their actual concept artwork through a rectangular aperture, covering each model change. Bricolage Grotesque is the locally bundled display family; Manrope remains the reading and interface family. The existing rose accent, neutral foundation, and project colours remain. Design variance 8, motion intensity 7, visual density 3. Native scrolling, readable copy, and direct navigation remain essential.

Approved direction: replace the homepage carousel with one continuous personal
story, rather than placing another animated showcase beneath it.

1. **Meet Hardik:** a local cutout of the standing camera portrait, two approved photographic windows, expressive name typography, and the Windows full-circle introduction form the opening. Work and resume are immediately reachable. Phones use the intact bench portrait.
2. **How I think and build:** photographic panes assemble as the story moves from customizing a PC to designing at Microsoft. His camera portrait accompanies his belief in helping stakeholders see through the same lens. Copy remains readable throughout.

Implementation guardrails: preserve native document
scrolling, deep links, and keyboard navigation; keep pinned moments short and
reversible; provide ordinary stacked content for reduced motion and smaller
screens; never require a loading gate, sound, or a completed animation to reach
work. Do not add a second homepage project catalogue. Reuse the current Three.js
renderer where suitable. GSAP ScrollTrigger coordinates the native scroll
choreography; CSS sticky positions the visuals. No scroll-smoothing or wheel
interception engine is used. (Superseded for the React rebuild: Lenis smooths

## Scope

- A portrait-led first-person introduction, Windows origin story, shared-perspective approach, selected work, personal interests, and contact form the homepage.
- On sufficiently large screens without reduced motion, native scrolling coordinates the foreground portrait, type, and photo windows; assembles the photographic panes; and expands project artwork over each model change. All transitions reverse with the scroll position.
- Project text uses a contrast-tested scrim, not a decorative card. Credits use appropriate per-project ink. The original personal photographs remain unchanged; one transparent foreground derivative is locally segmented and cropped for the opening. Facial features are not generated or retouched, and faces stay clear of display text.
- Every project has native text for role, problem, key decisions, and result, plus a direct reading link. Screens are real product work from Hardik's Figma case studies; targets are labelled as targets.
- Phones, shorter screens, and reduced motion use an ordinary static layout with a local image for every project. They do not initialize WebGL on a fresh visit.
- The Work page keeps its comparison catalogue. The homepage has one project sequence, not a duplicate catalogue or carousel controls.
- Earlier work (SMS Organizer, Bing Travel Map and Bing Trip Planning, 2023 to 2024 at Microsoft x Tech Mahindra) is listed only on the Work page, below the three featured projects, with full case study pages rebuilt from Hardik's previous portfolio.
- About leads with the full-circle story, working approach, values, and personal interests before career history. Personal copy uses details supplied by Hardik.
- About, Resume PDF, and email remain directly accessible. Project pages lead with the outcome and impact figures, then role, team, timeline, and scope; each key decision pairs short text with its screens, which follow the text on phones.
- Existing reading deep links remain valid and avoid WebGL initialization. Return and browser navigation preserve selected-project context.
- Daylight/evening themes, reduced motion, responsive framing, a semantic HTML reading experience, and an image fallback for graphics failure.
- The prior desk study remains available as a separate page and standalone build. Its assets and interaction code are not loaded by the exhibition.
- Additional photo sourcing, Instagram, confidential project imagery, detailed research case studies, and deployment remain deferred. The three supplied personal photographs are approved for this local iteration.
- No confidential product screenshots or invented research, screens, testimonials, or results.

## Anti-references

No literal room simulation as the main navigation. No unrelated floating props, fake product UI, copied portfolio compositions, generic feature-card grids, compulsory loading gates, or custom cursor. Three-dimensional movement must help browse the work. Graphical outcome studies must stay distinct from actual product evidence.

## Design Principles

- Project artwork carries the visual experience; native HTML carries navigation, identity, evidence, and reading.
- A small number of well-executed interactions is more valuable than a large collection of unrelated effects.
- Exploration is optional. Work is reachable from the first screen.
- Interviewers can compare projects by role and result, read a summary, get the resume, and make contact without interacting with 3D. Browser navigation and keyboard return paths should remain predictable.
- Responsive changes preserve readable information and a prominent selected exhibit, not an identical camera crop at every size.
- Preserve the resume's attribution and approximate metrics.
- Each homepage section must add distinct information. Do not repeat the same project artwork and outcomes in a second browsing layout below the exhibition.
- Do not reduce the person to their employer and project metrics. Keep the personal introduction visible on phones and preserve the distinction between personal context, practice, and project evidence.
- Home, End, arrow keys, and touch scrolling keep their native behavior. Escape dismisses feedback or returns from a reader to the story. Existing reading links and legacy exhibit hashes remain valid.
- Resolve links into pinned scenes against their static sequence wrappers, not the current viewport position of the sticky child. Remove transition layers and restore normal content when the cinematic media query no longer matches.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA. Support keyboard navigation, touch, system theme, reduced motion, and real links to all project summaries. Native content and local images keep the story usable without WebGL. Offscreen and reading-page rendering pauses. No animation gates reading or navigation. Do not request webcam, microphone, location, or device-orientation permission.
